# Search Strategy

- Topic: Knowledge retention across individual learning, teams, and organizations
- Review question: What mechanisms explain durable retention and retrieval of knowledge, and what practices preserve knowledge when people, tasks, or organizational conditions change?
- Search date: 2026-08-15
- Search sources: PubMed, publisher landing pages (Academy of Management, Wiley, Emerald, INFORMS, Science, Elsevier), arXiv, university repositories, and author publication pages
- Time window: No lower bound through 2026-08-15; foundational and current review papers prioritized
- Language limits: English
- Document type limits: Peer-reviewed reviews, meta-analyses, conceptual frameworks, and influential empirical studies; selected preprints only for the optional AI track

## Concept Clusters

- Individual retention
  - Keywords: long-term retention, retrieval practice, testing effect, spacing effect, distributed practice
  - Synonyms: durable learning, memory consolidation, repeated retrieval
- Team and organizational retention
  - Keywords: organizational memory, knowledge retention, knowledge loss, employee turnover, transactive memory
  - Synonyms: organizational forgetting, knowledge preservation, expertise loss, knowledge transfer
- Computational retention (adjacent/optional)
  - Keywords: continual learning, catastrophic forgetting, knowledge retention
  - Synonyms: lifelong learning, stability-plasticity

## Candidate Search Strings

1. `(knowledge retention OR long-term retention) AND (retrieval practice OR testing effect OR spacing OR distributed practice)`
2. `(organizational knowledge retention OR organizational memory OR knowledge loss OR organizational forgetting) AND (turnover OR transfer OR expertise OR review)`
3. `(transactive memory system) AND (team OR organization OR performance)`
4. `(continual learning OR catastrophic forgetting) AND (survey OR review OR knowledge retention)`

## Inclusion Criteria

1. Directly explains or tests mechanisms, interventions, measurement, or consequences of retaining and retrieving knowledge.
2. Provides original empirical evidence, a major conceptual framework, a meta-analysis, or a systematic/narrative review.
3. Has sufficient title/abstract/venue metadata to assess relevance and a stable publisher, repository, DOI, or PubMed record.
4. Foundational works are retained even when full text requires manual access.

## Exclusion Criteria

1. Uses “retention” only for employee retention, customer retention, data retention, or records compliance without studying knowledge persistence.
2. Discusses broad knowledge management without a substantive retention, memory, forgetting, or transfer component.
3. Non-scholarly commentary, duplicate records, theses when stronger peer-reviewed coverage exists, or papers with insufficient metadata.
4. AI continual-learning work is excluded from the core corpus and labeled optional because its unit of analysis differs materially from human and organizational knowledge.

## Notes

- Borderline rules: Knowledge transfer is included only when it bears directly on persistence across people or time. AI papers are optional, not evidence about human or organizational retention.
- Duplicate handling: Prefer the version of record; use an author manuscript only as the legal open-access copy.
- Access handling: `open` means a full-text copy was located and downloaded; `manual` means relevant but login, purchase, or institutional access may be required.
- Search limitation: This is a rigorous seed corpus, not an exhaustive database systematic review. Citation-chaining and subscription database searches would be required for exhaustiveness.
