# Included Studies and Reading Order

This corpus separates distinct levels of analysis. Do not treat evidence about a student's memory, a team's division of expertise, and an organization's routines as interchangeable.

## Start here: map the field

1. **Dunlosky et al. (2013), “Improving Students' Learning With Effective Learning Techniques.”** Broad evidence review of ten study techniques; practice testing and distributed practice receive the strongest ratings. DOI: [10.1177/1529100612453266](https://doi.org/10.1177/1529100612453266).
2. **Walsh & Ungson (1991), “Organizational Memory.”** Foundational framework: acquisition, retention facilities, and retrieval at organizational scale. DOI: [10.5465/AMR.1991.4278992](https://doi.org/10.5465/AMR.1991.4278992).
3. **Klammer & Gueldenberg (2019), “Unlearning and forgetting in organizations.”** Systematic review distinguishing intentionality and depth of knowledge loss. DOI: [10.1108/JKM-05-2018-0277](https://doi.org/10.1108/JKM-05-2018-0277).

## Individual learning and durable memory

4. **Cepeda et al. (2006), “Distributed practice in verbal recall tasks.”** Quantitative synthesis of 839 assessments from 317 experiments; establishes that optimal spacing depends on the desired retention interval. DOI: [10.1037/0033-2909.132.3.354](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.132.3.354).
5. **Roediger & Karpicke (2006), “The Power of Testing Memory.”** Major review of the testing effect and educational applications. DOI: [10.1111/j.1745-6916.2006.00012.x](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1745-6916.2006.00012.x).
6. **Karpicke & Roediger (2008), “The Critical Importance of Retrieval for Learning.”** Influential experiment showing repeated retrieval, not repeated study after successful recall, drives delayed recall. DOI: [10.1126/science.1152408](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1152408).
7. **Roediger & Butler (2011), “The critical role of retrieval practice in long-term retention.”** Compact mechanism-focused review, including feedback and transfer. DOI: [10.1016/j.tics.2010.09.003](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tics.2010.09.003).
8. **Karpicke & Roediger (2010), “Is expanding retrieval a superior method for learning text materials?”** Useful corrective: repeated retrieval helped, but expanding schedules did not outperform equal spacing in these experiments. DOI: [10.3758/MC.38.1.116](https://doi.org/10.3758/MC.38.1.116).

## Teams and organizations

9. **DeLong & Davenport (2003), “Better practices for retaining organizational knowledge.”** Practice-oriented bridge from risk identification to retention interventions. DOI: [10.1002/ert.10098](https://doi.org/10.1002/ert.10098).
10. **Lewis (2003), “Measuring transactive memory systems in the field.”** Develops a validated measure of specialization, credibility, and coordination in team memory. DOI: [10.1037/0021-9010.88.4.587](https://doi.org/10.1037/0021-9010.88.4.587).
11. **Lewis (2004), “Knowledge and Performance in Knowledge-Worker Teams.”** Longitudinal evidence about how transactive memory develops and affects performance. DOI: [10.1287/mnsc.1040.0257](https://doi.org/10.1287/mnsc.1040.0257).
12. **Mariano, Casey & Olivera (2020), “Organizational forgetting Part I.”** Review organizing forgetting into accidental/purposeful modes and knowledge depreciation, loss, and unlearning. Publisher page: [The Learning Organization](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0969647420000269).
13. **Castaneda, Manrique & Cuellar (2018), “Is organizational learning being absorbed by knowledge management?”** Large systematic review locating retention and transfer within the OL/KM relationship. DOI: [10.1108/JKM-01-2017-0041](https://doi.org/10.1108/JKM-01-2017-0041).

## Optional computational analogy—not part of the core evidence

14. **Parisi et al. (2019), “Continual Lifelong Learning with Neural Networks: A Review.”** A useful map of catastrophic forgetting and replay/plasticity mechanisms, but findings should not be generalized to people or firms. [arXiv:1802.07569](https://arxiv.org/abs/1802.07569).
15. **Wang et al. (2024), “A Comprehensive Survey of Continual Learning.”** Broad taxonomy of theory, methods, and applications. [arXiv:2302.00487](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.00487).

## Shortest defensible reading path

Read 1, 2, 4, 6, 10, and 3 in that order. This gives one broad learning review, the organizational foundation, quantitative evidence for spacing, causal evidence for retrieval, a team-memory measurement framework, and a modern review of organizational forgetting.
